A six-week online support program

Bite-sized format (10 mins per day)

The training is one-to-one, and all remote, using video calls and secure WhatsApp messages.

I’ll help you to build skills and confidence while completing ten minutes of tasks with your child each day, Monday to Friday, for six weeks.

Playful approach

I'll teach you how to introduce your child to word mapping. This innovative, accessible approach to phonics, developed by Emma-Hartnell Baker, introduces kids to speech sounds using fun characters, which they love.

Outcomes

Reading foundations for your child

By the end of the training, your child will:

  • Have begun their journey to becoming an expert ‘word mapper’ – developing orthographic knowledge.
  • Be reading and spelling with improved fluency – feeling more confident and motivated.
  • Have started to master the ability to isolate, blend, and segment words – phonemic awareness skills.
  • Learned a more flexible approach to reading unfamiliar words, so that they can begin to self-teach.

Many kids don't get these key foundations from the established approach to phonics (SSP). That's one reason that around 1 in 4 leave primary without meeting the expected reading standard. Word mapping is designed to plug those gaps.

A turning point for you, too

Through this training, you'll:

  • Better understand why reading and spelling have been challenging for your child.
  • Have developed skills in using a word-mapping approach with your child.
  • Be empowered to support your child’s learning journey and advocate for their needs.
  • Feel optimistic, because reading doesn’t have to be a battle – it can be something meaningful that you both enjoy.

How long will it take?

Six weeks is enough for many parents, but children learn at different paces and some may naturally need more time. It depends on their age and learning needs. Some parents appreciate lighter support after the reset too. We can discuss that together.

When is it time to act?

You don’t need to wait for a dyslexia diagnosis, or to do ‘more of the same’ at school if it isn’t working. Government data shows that for most kids, it won't.

Interventions for older children can and do help. But prevention is generally better for them and cheaper too. That's because, when they've secured the foundations of reading, children are less likely to need ongoing support.
